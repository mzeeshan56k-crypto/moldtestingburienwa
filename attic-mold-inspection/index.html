Roof sheathing mold: what an attic inspection looks for

An attic mold inspection examines the roof sheathing, rafters or trusses, insulation and ventilation in the space above your top floor. The inspector looks for mold growth, staining and frost or moisture marks, measures moisture in the wood, and traces how warm household air and outside air move through the space.

Attic mold in this region is usually a condensation problem rather than a roof leak. Warm, moist air from the house rises into the attic through gaps around light fixtures, the hatch, plumbing stacks and wiring. On cold nights, that moisture condenses on the underside of the roof deck, which is the coldest surface in the attic. Repeated over a long wet season, the result is widespread staining and growth. If you're comparing options for professional mold testing, attic checks are a common starting point.

How a Burien attic mold inspection company tests your home

The inspector enters through the hatch and surveys the attic, staying on framing and avoiding damage to insulation.

  • Visual mapping. Growth patterns tell a story. Even darkening across the north-facing roof deck points to condensation. Localized staining around a vent or chimney points to a leak.
  • Moisture readings. Pin meters measure moisture content in sheathing and framing to determine whether growth is active or old.
  • Ventilation assessment. Soffit intakes are checked for blockage by insulation, ridge or roof vents are located, and the balance between intake and exhaust is evaluated.
  • Exhaust ducts. Bathroom, kitchen and dryer ducts are followed to confirm they exit the roof or wall rather than ending in the attic.
  • Air leakage. Disturbed or thin insulation, gaps around the hatch, recessed lights and top plates are noted as sources of warm, moist air.
  • Thermal imaging. From inside the house, an infrared camera can show where heat and air are escaping into the attic.

Surface samples may be taken to confirm growth or document it for a sale. For homes where the attic problem is paired with musty odors indoors, consider a full mold inspection as well.

Signs you should book attic mold inspection near you

  • Dark roof sheathing. Black or gray staining on the underside of roof boards, especially on the north side.
  • Frost on nails. In cold weather, nail tips poking through the deck may show frost or rust streaks.
  • Damp insulation. Matted or compressed insulation signals moisture from above or below.
  • Musty smell at the hatch. A stale odor when you open the attic access.
  • Bath fan ducts ending in the attic. A duct that's disconnected or simply ends in insulation dumps steam directly onto the roof deck.
  • Blocked soffit vents. Insulation pushed against the eaves blocks the intake air that keeps the attic dry.
  • Ceiling stains on the top floor. Stains near exterior walls can come from attic moisture dripping down.
  • Ice-cold top-floor rooms. Poor insulation and air sealing increase both heat loss and condensation.

What causes the problems attic mold inspection uncovers?

Three conditions combine to cause most attic mold in Burien. First, moist indoor air leaks upward, carried by the same stack effect that pulls crawl space air into the house. Showers, cooking, laundry and even breathing add moisture to that air. Second, bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans are sometimes vented into the attic, or their ducts have come loose. Third, ventilation is out of balance, often because insulation added over the years now blocks the soffit vents.

The region's long, cool, damp season means attic surfaces rarely dry out between condensation events. Heavily shaded roofs on wooded lots, common in Seahurst, stay cold and wet longer. Roof leaks around flashing, chimneys and skylights cause localized problems as well.

What can you check yourself first?

  • From the hatch, use a flashlight to look at the underside of the roof boards for dark staining.
  • Run each bathroom fan and go outside to see whether air exits through a roof or wall cap.
  • Check that the attic hatch has weatherstripping and insulation on top.
  • Look at the eaves from outside. Soffit vents should be visible and unobstructed.

Pro tipA fan that's loud but weak often has a crushed or disconnected duct. That's one of the most common and cheapest fixes for attic moisture.

When do you need emergency attic mold inspection?

Attic mold is rarely an emergency on its own, but call promptly if there's an active roof leak dripping into insulation, if a storm has damaged the roof, or if ceiling drywall is sagging from water. Place a bucket, move belongings and arrange a temporary roof cover. Wet insulation should be removed and the area dried before mold spreads further.

How is attic mold usually corrected?

Fixing attic mold means stopping the moisture first and dealing with existing growth second. Cleaning the sheathing without changing airflow just resets the clock until the next wet season.

  • Route exhaust outside. Bathroom, kitchen and dryer ducts are connected and run to a roof or wall cap with a damper. Fans that move too little air may be replaced.
  • Air seal the ceiling. Gaps around light fixtures, wiring holes, plumbing stacks, top plates and the attic hatch are sealed so less household moisture reaches the attic.
  • Restore intake ventilation. Baffles are installed at the eaves to hold insulation back from soffit vents and keep an air channel open under the roof deck.
  • Balance exhaust ventilation. Ridge or roof vents are checked so the amount of exhaust matches the intake.
  • Treat existing growth. A remediation contractor may HEPA vacuum, clean or encapsulate affected sheathing, and replace boards only where damage is structural.

Insulation that got wet or contaminated is usually replaced after air sealing, which also improves comfort and energy use in the rooms below. Since many of these items are inexpensive individually, an inspection that ranks them by impact helps you prioritize. A follow-up check after the first cold, wet stretch confirms the attic is staying dry, and it's a good time to look at the crawl space too, since both are driven by the same airflow through the house.

Attic Mold Inspection FAQs for Burien homeowners

Is attic mold a problem if I never go up there?

It can be. Growth weakens over time, affects air drawn into the house, and is routinely flagged by buyers' inspectors during a sale.

Is staining always active mold?

Not always. Old staining can remain after conditions improve. Moisture readings help tell active from inactive.

Will more ventilation fix it?

Ventilation helps, but sealing air leaks from the house and routing exhaust fans outside are usually just as important.

Do I need a new roof?

Usually not. Most attic mold affects the surface of the sheathing. Structural damage is less common but should be checked.

How did my bath fan end up venting into the attic?

Sometimes it was installed that way, and sometimes ducts come loose over time. It's a common finding in older homes.

Can I spray it myself?

Spraying doesn't fix the cause. The moisture source and ventilation need to be addressed or growth returns.
